A work quota is the amount of work a member owes the community — usually stated in hours a week or a month. Grounds, maintenance, cleaning shared rooms, cooking on the rota, administration, sitting on a circle.

Two to six hours a week is typical in a settled community with private finances. Income-sharing communities ask for far more because the work _is_ the economy: Twin Oaks sets 38.5 hours a week, covering both its businesses and its domestic areas.

## Counted, or trusted?

Communities split into three rough camps, and which one you are joining matters more than the number.

**Counted.** Hours are logged, usually as <Gloss term="labour-credit">labour credits</Gloss>. Fair, transparent, administratively heavy, and prone to feeling like a timesheet in a household.

**Stated but not counted.** A quota exists in the <Gloss term="agreements">agreements</Gloss>; nobody audits it. Most common, and it works exactly as long as the group is willing to have an awkward conversation with somebody who is not pulling their weight.

**Neither.** No stated expectation. This does not mean less work happens; it means the work lands on whoever cannot stand to see it undone, which within a few years is a specific and resentful list of names.

## The question worth asking

Not "what is the quota?" but **"what happens when someone does not meet it?"**

Every community has an answer and most have not written it down. A rule with no stated consequence is a preference, and members work out which is which inside a year.

<Callout type="caveat">

A quota measures the work somebody thought to put on a list. It systematically misses <Gloss term="invisible-work">invisible work</Gloss> — noticing, remembering, smoothing — which is why a community can be fully compliant with its own quota and still have three people quietly holding it together.

</Callout>
