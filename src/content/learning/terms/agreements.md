Agreements are the written rules a community has consented to and can point at. Not aspirations, not a statement of values — the specific, checkable text that says who decides what, what each member owes, and what happens when somebody does not deliver it.

The distinction from **values** is the one that matters. "We value transparency" is a value. "Minutes are published within three days, and any member may see the accounts on request" is an agreement. The first cannot be broken, only disappointed. The second can be broken, which is exactly what makes it useful: a group can notice, name it, and do something.

## What a working set covers

Communities differ enormously in what they share, but the agreements that keep appearing in the ones that last are unglamorous and procedural: how a decision is made and by whom, what money each person owes and when, how a person joins, how a person leaves and what they take with them, and what happens when an agreement is broken.

That last one is the most commonly missing. A rule with no stated consequence is a preference, and everybody learns which is which within about a year.

## An example

The clearest test is the one to apply on a visit: ask to read the agreements, and ask when they were last reviewed. A community that can hand you a current document has a working system. One that describes the feeling but cannot find the text has an intention — which is not worthless, but it is not the same thing, and it will not help when two members disagree about what was decided in 2019.

<Callout type="caveat">
Agreements decay quietly. The usual pattern is not that they are broken but that they stop being read: new members never see them, the people who wrote them leave, and the document describes a community that no longer exists. A review date written into the agreements themselves is the cheapest defence.
</Callout>
