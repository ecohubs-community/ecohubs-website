A reserve fund — sinking fund, capital reserve, depreciation account, the names vary — is money collected monthly and held against work that is certain to be needed and uncertain in its timing. Roofs, boilers, resurfacing, the septic system, the lift.

It is the least visible part of a community's finances and the most predictive.

## Why the low-dues community may be the expensive one

The best budget data available in this field, from a study of 20 American cohousing communities comprising 611 units, found that **roughly a third of the average annual budget goes into savings** rather than being spent that year.

Read that against a community advertising unusually low monthly charges. Either it owns very little in common, or it is not funding its reserve — and a roof does not stop ageing because nobody budgeted for it. The money is not saved; it is deferred, and it returns as a <Gloss term="special-levy">special levy</Gloss> that lands on whoever happens to be a member that year.

## What to ask

Three questions, and a community with its house in order answers all three without looking anything up.

**What is in the fund now?** A number, not a reassurance.

**When was it last reviewed, and against what?** The good answer names a survey or a schedule of major works with dates and estimated costs. The weak answer is "we put a bit aside".

**When did you last raise a levy, and how much was it?** "Never" is a good answer only from a community old enough to have needed one.

<Callout type="caveat">

A reserve fund is also a governance question, not only a financial one. Ask who may spend it and on what — a fund that the group can vote to raid for something enjoyable is not a reserve, it is a savings account with an optimistic label.

</Callout>
