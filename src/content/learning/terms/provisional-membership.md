Provisional membership — associate, candidate, trial member, the names vary — is the stage between visiting and belonging. It lasts months rather than weeks, carries real obligations and usually a financial commitment, and is explicitly reversible by either side.

It is the most informative stage of the whole process, and the one people rush.

## What makes it worth having

Anyone can be pleasant for three weeks. Provisional membership is long enough to include a disagreement, a rota you resented, a meeting that ran late, and a week when you were unwell. That is the material both sides actually need.

It also protects the joiner. Withdrawing at this stage is the process working, not a failure — which is exactly why the terms of withdrawal matter.

## The questions that reveal the design

**What may a provisional member decide?** The answer describes how the community thinks about power in one sentence. Full voice and no vote is a coherent position; so is a vote on everything except membership and money. No stated position is not.

**What have you paid in, and what would you get back?** Ask before you need to know. A provisional stage requiring a substantial non-refundable payment is a decision dressed as a trial.

**How does it end?** A stated review date and a named decision-maker, or a vague sense that it will become permanent when it feels right. The second is how somebody spends three years as a provisional member.

<Callout type="caveat">

A long provisional stage can quietly become a second class of resident: doing the work, paying the charges, and never getting a say. If the community has people who have been provisional for years, ask why — the answer is usually about money or about conflict avoidance, and both are worth knowing.

</Callout>
