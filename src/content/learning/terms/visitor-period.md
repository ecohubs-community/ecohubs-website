A visitor period is a fixed stretch — commonly one to four weeks — during which somebody considering membership lives on the community's ordinary terms: on the work rota, at the meals, and usually in at least one meeting.

It is not a holiday and it is not yet an application. It is the stage where both sides find out what they are actually dealing with.

## What it is for

For the community, it is the only realistic way to learn how somebody behaves when they are tired, when a decision goes against them, and when nobody is being impressive. For the visitor, it is the only way to discover whether the texture of the place — the noise, the meetings, the being known — is one they want.

The best-designed periods are deliberately unglamorous. A visit scheduled around a festival and a good dinner tells nobody anything.

## What to look for

**Is it a fixed length, with a stated end?** An open-ended stay quietly becomes unpaid labour with no decision at the end of it.

**What do you pay, and what do you do?** Both should be stated in advance.

**Is there an interview, and what does it cover?** Good processes ask about conflict rather than belief.

**When and how is the decision made?** Twin Oaks, for instance, runs a three-week period, then an input round in which every member records accept, visit again, or reject, with a membership team deciding. Knowing the mechanism ahead of time makes the outcome far easier to receive.

<Callout type="caveat">

Ask whether you may come back a second time, in a different season. A rural community in July and the same community in February are different places, and the visitor period is the cheapest chance you will get to see both.

</Callout>
