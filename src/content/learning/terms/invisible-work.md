Invisible work is everything a community needs that nobody thought to put on a list. Remembering that the insurance renews. Noticing that a new member has not been included in anything for a fortnight. Absorbing the awkwardness after a difficult meeting. Being the person who always makes the tea, and the person everyone tells things to.

It is real work, it takes real time, and it is the single most reliable source of quiet resentment in community life.

## Why it goes uncounted

A <Gloss term="work-quota">work quota</Gloss> counts tasks that can be described in advance. Invisible work is defined by responding to something that has just happened, which makes it hard to schedule and impossible to rota.

There is a second reason, less comfortable. This work tends to be done by people who are good at noticing, and it distributes itself along much the same lines it does everywhere else — by gender, by temperament, by who was raised to feel responsible for the room. A community that does not name this does not thereby avoid it.

## What honest communities do

**Name it out loud.** Simply having the phrase available changes what can be discussed.

**Count some of it.** Convening a circle, welcoming new members, and holding the calendar can all be made into stated roles with hours attached.

**Rotate the roles that accrete authority.** <Gloss term="facilitation">Facilitation</Gloss> is the obvious one, but so is holding the accounts and holding the keys.

**Ask, twice.** Ask who does the invisible work here, and then ask somebody else the same question. The answers rarely match, and the gap tells you more about the community than its constitution does.

<Callout type="caveat">

Beware the compliment that fixes it in place. "She's just so good at that" is how a community explains to itself why one person has done a job nobody wanted for eleven years.

</Callout>
