A domain is the boundary of a <Gloss term="circle">circle</Gloss>'s or a role's authority: the decisions it may make by itself, and the point past which it has to come back to the wider group. It is the difference between delegation and consultation, and it is the single most common thing missing when a community says it has adopted <Gloss term="sociocracy">sociocracy</Gloss> and nothing has got faster.

## What a real domain states

- **Subject.** What the circle is responsible for — the buildings, the land, the welcome of new members.
- **A budget, or another concrete limit.** A number it may spend without asking. This is what turns a domain from a description into an authority, and it is the part most often omitted.
- **What it may not do.** The decisions that look like they belong here and do not — usually anything that changes an agreement, commits the community long-term, or affects who is a member.
- **What it reports, and to whom.** Delegated authority without a reporting line is not delegation, it is disappearance.

## The test

If forming a circle has not made the general meeting shorter, the domain was never real.

This is unusually easy to check, and it catches almost every case of the pattern being adopted in name only. A maintenance circle with no spending authority still brings every repair to the whole group for approval, which means the community has added a meeting rather than removed one. Members experience this as sociocracy being bureaucratic. What is actually happening is that nobody was willing to let go of the money.

## The opposite failure

Domains can also be drawn too wide. A circle that can commit the community to a five-year lease is making a constitutional decision inside an operational structure, and the group will only find out when the bill arrives. The corrective is the same as everywhere else in governance: name the limit explicitly and default an unclear case upward, to the wider group, rather than downward.

<Callout type="caveat">

A written domain distributes authority. It does not distribute knowledge. A circle whose members are the only people who understand the heating system holds informal power well beyond its stated boundary, and no amount of careful drafting touches that. Handover and documentation are what address it.

</Callout>
