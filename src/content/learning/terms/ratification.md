Ratification is the gap between a decision passing and a decision being in force. A group that has none is not doing anything wrong — it has simply chosen that the vote closing and the decision taking effect are the same moment. What matters is that the choice was made on purpose.

Most communities never think about this until the first time a decision passes on a Thursday, someone acts on it on Friday, and on Monday the group realises that four members were on a retreat with no signal and never saw it. The result was correct by the rules. It was also not what anyone wanted.

## The four shapes it takes

- **None.** The decision takes effect when voting closes. Right for anything reversible, and right for most day-to-day matters — a hold on the compost rota is bureaucracy with no purchase.
- **Cooling-off.** A fixed delay before it takes effect, with nothing else required. See <Gloss term="cooling-off-period">cooling-off period</Gloss>. It buys time for a mistake to be noticed without giving anyone new power.
- **Facilitator sign-off.** A named person confirms the process was followed — that the <Gloss term="quorum">quorum</Gloss> was met, that the wording that passed is the wording that was voted on, that objections were handled. Not a second opinion on the merits, and it goes wrong exactly when it becomes one.
- **Veto window.** Members may still object during a set period after voting, and a sustained objection stops it. See <Gloss term="veto-window">veto window</Gloss>.

## What each one is actually protecting against

They are not interchangeable, and choosing between them means naming the risk. A cooling-off period protects against haste. Sign-off protects against a procedurally broken result being treated as valid. A veto window protects the people who were not in the room. If a group cannot say which of those it is worried about, the honest answer is probably "none" — and adding a hold anyway just teaches everyone that decisions are not really decisions.

<Callout type="caveat">

Ratification is the phase most likely to become theatre. A sign-off that is never withheld, or a veto window nobody has used in four years, looks like a safeguard and functions as a delay. The test is whether anyone can name a time it changed an outcome. If nobody can, it is either unnecessary or it is not really available to the people it was meant to protect.

</Callout>

A ratification step also changes what the record says. A decision that passed but was not confirmed reads _not ratified_ rather than _failed_ — different things, and worth keeping distinct, because one of them says the group disagreed and the other says the process stopped it.
