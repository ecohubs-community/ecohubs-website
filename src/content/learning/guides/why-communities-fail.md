faq:
  - question: Is this a list of reasons communities fail?
    answer: >-
      No. Those exist, and most of them are essays. This is a catalogue: each
      pattern has its own page with the signs, the mechanism, what to change,
      and what to do if it is already happening. It is meant to be used during
      a difficult year, not read once.
  - question: Where do the patterns come from?
    answer: >-
      Twenty-four of them from the
      [RCOS stress tests](https://rcos.ecohubs.community/articles/rcos-stress-tests),
      which document failure patterns with the conditions that produce them.
      Every one of those pages links to its stress test, so you can read the
      specification behind the plain-language version.
      The twenty-fifth — sub-groups with undefined autonomy — is a pattern the
      standard does not yet catalogue, and that page says so rather than
      leaving the gap for somebody to notice later.
  - question: Do you name real communities?
    answer: >-
      Only where they published the material themselves or a court or regulator
      did, and every critical claim is attributed to that source rather than
      asserted. Where we draw on advisory work, the community is not named. Some
      patterns have no citable case at all, and those pages say that too —
      [how this is written](/learn/how-this-is-written) sets out the rules.
  - question: We are already in trouble. Where do I start?
    answer: >-
      With the symptom, not the category. Every lesson leads with stabilisation
      and follows with prevention, because a community in the middle of a live
      failure does not need to be told what it should have done three years ago.
